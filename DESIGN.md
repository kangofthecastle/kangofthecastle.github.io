# Visual direction

The site is a collection of personal software, grounded in Vancouver. A harbour print is the identity anchor. Project imagery stays faithful to the actual software.

## Shared rules

- Warm paper (#f3f0e8), deep green ink (#263739), oxidized red (#a94332). Red marks emphasis and interaction, not whole text panels. Project imagery supplies secondary colors.
- Instrument Serif for names and page headings, italic for editorial emphasis. DM Sans for explanations and navigation. Monospace only for small labels, dates and technology lists. Fonts are self-hosted; licenses are in public/fonts.
- A 1280px content measure, generous gutters, fine rules, and an asymmetric two-column spread. On small screens each spread becomes one ordered column. No heavy borders, arbitrary rotations or offset shadows.
- Project visuals use actual captures. Toybox uses an explicitly labeled utility illustration with the original sprite; it is not an invented screenshot.
- Motion carries the project story: a layered harbour, an immersive desktop gallery, a bullet-to-gold illustration using original HUBRIS sprites, A-Game’s boat-to-galaxy journey, and an unfolding FreeCAT workspace. A shared background and camera movement connect the chapters. Four direct chapter buttons let the reader jump through the pinned gallery; project detail links remain usable in each active scene. Narrow screens retain normal document flow with larger camera movement and colored chapter backgrounds.
- Reduced motion and JavaScript-free browsing use the complete static layout. The gallery is enabled only at widths of at least 900px and heights of at least 720px, where the full copy fits. Only the active chapter participates in keyboard focus; chapter selectors remain available. Screenshot selectors are keyboard-operable buttons with a pressed state.

## Editorial constraint

Preserve the user's wording. Flag weak copy separately, propose exact replacements, and obtain the user's approval before changing it.

## References

Jackie Zhang: commitment to one illustrated material world. Nayn: a complete metaphor whose artwork and interactions agree. Maxime Heckel: careful technical craft and controlled hierarchy. The site takes those principles without copying their artwork or interface.
